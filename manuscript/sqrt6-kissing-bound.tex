\begin{abstract}
Let $\tau_n$ denote the kissing number in Euclidean dimension $n$.  We give an elementary proof that
\[
\tau_n\leq (\sqrt{6})^n
\qquad(n\geq 1).
\]
The constant $\sqrt{6}$ is optimal among bounds of the form $\tau_n\leq \alpha^n$ required to hold in every dimension, since $\tau_2=6$.  The argument combines the elementary spherical-cap packing bound with a two-step recurrence for cap-volume fractions.  An accompanying Lean~4/Mathlib development certifies the bound and its optimality by an alternative Euclidean-volume argument.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement of the bound}

For $n\geq 1$, let $\tau_n$ be the largest number of nonoverlapping unit balls in $\R^n$ that can simultaneously touch a fixed unit ball.  Equivalently, $\tau_n$ is the largest cardinality of a subset of the unit sphere $S^{n-1}$ whose pairwise angular distances are at least $\pi/3$.

The following answers the universal-base question that motivated this note.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every integer $n\geq 1$,
\[
\tau_n\leq (\sqrt{6})^n.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:base}
The least real number $\alpha>0$ such that
\[
\tau_n\leq \alpha^n
\qquad\text{for every }n\geq 1
\]
is $\sqrt{6}$.  Equivalently,
\[
\sup_{n\geq 1}\tau_n^{1/n}=\sqrt{6}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\section{Proof}

For $n\geq 2$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cn}
 c_n:=
 \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/6}\sin^{n-2}t\,dt}
      {\displaystyle\int_0^\pi\sin^{n-2}t\,dt}.
\end{equation}
This is the normalized surface measure of a spherical cap of angular radius $\pi/6$ in $S^{n-1}$.  Indeed, polar coordinates about the center of the cap give the surface element as a constant multiple of $\sin^{n-2}t\,dt$, and the constant cancels in the ratio \eqref{eq:cn}.

If the centers of two such caps have angular distance at least $\pi/3$, then their interiors are disjoint.  The boundaries have surface measure zero, so every kissing configuration gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packing}
 \tau_n c_n\leq 1.
\end{equation}
We next obtain a uniform lower bound for $c_n$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:recurrence}
For every $n\geq 2$,
\[
 c_{n+2}>\frac{c_n}{6}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $m=n-2$ and set
\[
 I_m:=\int_0^{\pi/6}\sin^m t\,dt,
 \qquad
 J_m:=\int_0^\pi\sin^m t\,dt,
\]
so that $c_n=I_m/J_m$.  Integration by parts gives
\begin{align}
 I_{m+2}
 &=\frac{m+1}{m+2}I_m
   -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{(m+2)2^{m+2}}.\label{eq:Im}\\
 J_{m+2}
 &=\frac{m+1}{m+2}J_m.\label{eq:Jm}
\end{align}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crecurrence}
 c_{n+2}
 =c_n-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{(m+1)2^{m+2}J_m}.
\end{equation}

It remains to bound $I_m$ from below.  With the substitution $u=\sin t$,
\[
 I_m=\int_0^{1/2}\frac{u^m}{\sqrt{1-u^2}}\,du.
\]
For $0\leq u\leq 1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elementary}
 \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-u^2}}\geq 1+\frac{u^2}{2}.
\end{equation}
To see this, both sides are nonnegative and
\[
 (1-u^2)\left(1+\frac{u^2}{2}\right)^2
 =1-\frac{3u^4}{4}-\frac{u^6}{4}
 \leq 1.
\]
It follows that
\begin{align*}
 I_m
 &\geq \int_0^{1/2}u^m\left(1+\frac{u^2}{2}\right)\,du\\
 &=\frac{1}{2^m(m+1)}
   \left(\frac12+\frac{m+1}{16(m+3)}\right)\\
 &\geq \frac{25}{48\,2^m(m+1)}.
\end{align*}
Since $\sqrt{3}<26/15$, we have
\[
 \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{10}<\frac{13}{25}<\frac{25}{48},
\]
and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ilower}
 I_m>\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{10(m+1)2^m}.
\end{equation}
Using $J_m>0$, inequality \eqref{eq:Ilower} yields
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{3}}{(m+1)2^{m+2}J_m}
 <\frac56\frac{I_m}{J_m}
 =\frac56c_n.
\]
Substitution into \eqref{eq:crecurrence} proves
$c_{n+2}>c_n/6$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
First suppose that $n$ is even.  Since $c_2=1/6$, Lemma~\ref{lem:recurrence} gives
\[
 c_n\geq 6^{-n/2}.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:packing}, this implies
\[
 \tau_n\leq \frac1{c_n}\leq 6^{n/2}=(\sqrt{6})^n.
\]

For odd $n\geq 5$, direct integration gives
\[
 c_5=\frac12-\frac{9\sqrt{3}}{32}.
\]
The estimate $\sqrt{3}<26/15$ implies $c_5>1/80$, whereas
\[
 (\sqrt{6})^5=36\sqrt{6}>80
\]
because $\sqrt{6}>20/9$.  Thus $c_5>6^{-5/2}$, and another application of Lemma~\ref{lem:recurrence} gives
\[
 c_n>6^{-n/2}
 \qquad\text{for every odd }n\geq 5.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:packing} again yields the desired bound.

It remains to consider $n=1$ and $n=3$.  In dimension one, $\tau_1=2<\sqrt{6}$.  In dimension three,
\[
 c_3=\frac{2-\sqrt{3}}{4}>\frac1{15},
\]
where the last inequality follows from $\sqrt{3}<26/15$.  Hence \eqref{eq:packing} gives $\tau_3<15$, so the integrality of $\tau_3$ gives $\tau_3\leq 14$.  Finally,
\[
 14<6\sqrt{6}=(\sqrt{6})^3,
\]
because $\sqrt{6}>7/3$.  This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:base}]
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} shows that $\alpha=\sqrt{6}$ works.  Conversely, $\tau_2=6$: six equally spaced points on $S^1$ form a kissing configuration, while pairwise angular separation at least $\pi/3$ allows at most six points around the circle.  Therefore every universal base $\alpha$ satisfies
\[
 6=\tau_2\leq \alpha^2,
\]
and hence $\alpha\geq\sqrt{6}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The argument is deliberately elementary and is not intended to compete asymptotically with the best high-dimensional spherical-code bounds.  Its point is that $\sqrt{6}$ is the optimal constant in a dimension-uniform estimate having the particularly simple form $\tau_n\leq \alpha^n$ with no additional prefactor.
\end{remark}

\section{Scope of the Lean certificate}\label{sec:lean}

The accompanying repository is
\begin{center}
\url{https://github.com/sebastienpalcoux/sqrt6-kissing-bound}.
\end{center}
It pins Lean~4.33.1 and Mathlib revision
\texttt{0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474}.
The formal development defines a finite kissing configuration to be a set of unit vectors whose distinct members have inner product at most $1/2$. It proves, from exactly these hypotheses, that its cardinality is at most $(\sqrt6)^n$ for every $n\ge1$. The kissing number is then constructed as the greatest realizable cardinality, and both its realization and maximality are proved. An explicit regular hexagon establishes $\tau_2=6$ and the optimality of the universal base.

The formal proof uses disjoint measurable bodies inside the open $30^\circ$ cones associated with the configuration. Writing $V_n$ for the volume of the unit ball in $\R^n$, an inscribed bicone has volume
\[
 \frac{V_{n-1}}{n2^{n-1}}.
\]
Fubini's theorem and exact one-dimensional integration establish this formula. For $n\ge6$, the inequalities $V_n\le V_{n-1}$ and $n2^{n-1}\le(\sqrt6)^n$ finish the argument. Dimensions $1$ through $5$ are treated separately; the three- and four-dimensional cases use explicit profiles with exactly computed volumes. No exact three- or four-dimensional kissing number is assumed.

Thus the certificate proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:base} unconditionally, but it does not certify every intermediate identity of the cap-integral proof in Section~2. In particular, the general identity identifying the spherical-cap area with the sine-integral ratio~\eqref{eq:cn} remains outside the formalization. The analytic recurrence and a chord-based lower estimate are verified separately as companion lemmas. The repository file \texttt{FORMALIZATION\_SCOPE.md} describes the proof dependencies and the low-dimensional constructions.

The command \texttt{bash scripts/check.sh} builds the complete development, scans the project for proof holes and new axioms, and audits the axiom dependencies of all project declarations. Only the standard foundational axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound} are allowed. These checks establish formal validity relative to Lean and the pinned Mathlib development; they do not replace an independent human assessment of the manuscript.
